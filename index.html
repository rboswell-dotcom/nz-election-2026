How MMP works

Two votes

Every voter gets two votes: an electorate vote for their local MP, and a party vote that determines each party's total seat share in Parliament. The party vote is the more important of the two for the overall result.

The 5% threshold

A party must win either 5% of the party vote, or at least one electorate seat, to enter Parliament. Parties that don't clear this threshold receive no seats even if they received hundreds of thousands of votes. This is why smaller parties often campaign hard in specific electorates — ACT in Epsom, for example.

Overhang seats

If a party wins more electorate seats than their party vote entitles them to, Parliament temporarily expands to accommodate them. In 2023, Te Pāti Māori won 6 electorate seats but only 3.1% of the party vote (entitling them to ~4 seats), creating a 3-seat overhang and a 123-seat Parliament.

Coalition governments

Because MMP produces proportional results, no single party usually wins a majority alone. Parties negotiate coalition agreements after the election. The current government is a three-party coalition — National, ACT, and NZ First — holding 67 of 123 seats.
